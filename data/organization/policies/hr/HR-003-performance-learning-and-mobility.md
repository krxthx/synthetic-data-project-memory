# HR-003: Performance, Learning and Internal Mobility

Policy owner: Maya Desai, Chief People Officer  
Operational owner: Daniel Wu, HR Operations Director  
Version: 1.0  
Status: Current  
Effective: 2026-03-01  
Review: 2027-03-01

## Purpose

This policy connects performance expectations, development and internal opportunity while preventing isolated metrics or undocumented impressions from becoming employment decisions. It applies to employees in Meridian Group's standard performance cycle. Local processes may add requirements but may not remove evidence, review or non-retaliation safeguards.

## Goals and ongoing feedback

Managers and employees establish a small set of outcomes, role responsibilities and behavior expectations. Goals identify how success will be evidenced and are updated when priorities materially change. A later target must not be applied retroactively. Regular check-ins record decisions, support requested, constraints and development actions; they are not intended to become verbatim transcripts of every conversation.

Feedback should be specific, timely and connected to observable work. Project feedback may inform an organizational review, but the receiving manager evaluates the contributor's role, decision authority, dependencies and period of involvement. Anonymous or second-hand concerns require corroboration before they support a material conclusion.

## Performance decisions

Ratings consider agreed outcomes, quality, judgment, collaboration, conduct, learning and relevant context across the review period. Utilization, office attendance, message volume, code activity, sales activity or other telemetry cannot be the sole basis for a rating. AI-generated summaries may organize evidence but cannot determine a rating, infer protected characteristics or replace accountable manager review.

A significant performance concern is raised promptly rather than saved for the annual review. The employee receives the expectation, observed gap, available support, review period and consequences. Accommodation, approved leave and documented dependency failures are considered before conclusions are drawn. Calibration checks consistency; it must not impose a predetermined distribution of ratings.

Employees may request a review through the People Partner when evidence is materially wrong, relevant context was excluded or a conflict exists. Disagreement alone does not invalidate a rating, but the review outcome and rationale are retained.

## Learning and internal mobility

Development plans may include mentoring, formal learning, stretch work, role shadowing and skill practice. Mandatory learning is completed during working time. Completion of a course does not by itself demonstrate proficiency, and lack of access to a development opportunity must not be treated as lack of interest.

Employees may apply for posted internal roles without prior manager permission. Recruiting informs the current manager only at the defined stage or with the employee's consent, subject to local requirements. Hiring decisions use published criteria and documented evidence. A current manager may propose a transition period for a critical responsibility but cannot indefinitely block a transfer. The handover identifies knowledge, access, deliverables and an agreed release date.

## Records and responsibilities

Employees maintain goals and provide relevant evidence. Managers give feedback, make documented decisions and protect confidentiality. HR Operations owns the cycle and access controls. People Partners review disputes and delayed transfers. Performance records follow IT-003 classification and retention requirements; medical information remains in the accommodation process, not the performance file.
